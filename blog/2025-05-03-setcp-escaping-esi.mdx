## Hook

It was a dark and stormy meeting. All eyes were on you as you hunted for the elusive spreadsheet &mdash; the one you knew, deep in your soul, hadn't been touched since the last time this meeting wasted your life.

There you are, trapped in the labyrinth, traversing the network drive directory structure trying to locate which folder that goddam spreadsheet lives in. No luck in the *NAS*ty hierarchy of mostly empty and deeply nested folders and some times simply unadulterated digital dumpsters....then you jump to the equally nasty **_set_** of Teams folders, a maze of its own, of Teams everyone and their mother think you should be on and apparently can't come up with an original name between themselves...or, is it on SharePoint? Or Microsoft OneDrive? Is it my personal OneDrive or the organizational OneDrive? Or, absolutely worst of all, someone's C drive, who, conveniently, is on vacation "with spotty connectivity".

:::note

I have a deep-seated issue with Microsoft's use of the word Team.

:::

The most soul-crushing part of all this? That spreadsheet exists for no other reason than this: the applications the corporate gods deemed "synergistically cross-functional", and "cohesively integrated", are usually such a nightmare to use that the most savvy among us are only really adept at extracting the useful data into...you guessed it..._that fucking spreadsheet_...the very thing the monstrous app was supposed to eliminate in the first place. But which spreadsheet is the latest data in? And where the hell is it hiding?

## The Hall of Shame

I want to begin this journey by priming your mind with a hard truth: the hall of shame is really flipping long, and _very_ cluttered. Many of our supposedly beloved applications are likely to find themselves on the hall walls, though we may not speak of them here, and though they rightly deserve criticism. I will limit the remainder of this screed to a relatively manageable length.

:::note

Why "supposedly beloved", you might say to yourself? I say it this way because there are some apologists for these hall of shame superstars who have their identities and resumes so tightly coupled to these applications that they, naturally, feel it's in their best interest to defend the offenders of sanity. It's a faux love. It's really a love of self, more than a love of any particular application. An instinct for survival cannot be faulted.

:::

### The Shame-ers

- SAP
- Dynamics
- Deltek

### What do they have in common

- very poor UI/UX
- one size fits most
- more config and consulting than actual productivity tools
- far too much information displayed
- far too many buttons with dubious purpose
- by 100% us 5% &larr; I really don't like to use this smaller percentage...it's impossible to truly quantify. But there is a qualitative _feeling_ that much of the application isn't being use. Now, if we dial that in a bit, much of the **UI** isn't being use, that's a sign.

My own personal experience with trying to choose one for my own business...

- very expensive. Features over function(?).
- tons of features, but was hard to know which would be useful, if any.
- Much of these applications would force us to do things completely differently (although I'm not opposed to this, so this is a really terrible argument against them).
